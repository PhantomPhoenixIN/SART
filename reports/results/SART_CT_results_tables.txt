METHODOLOGY AND EXPERIMENT RECORD
=================================

1. Research question and status
--------------------------------
Question: Does structure-guided, region-adaptive recurrent computation improve
functional code translation or compute efficiency relative to a standard
transformer and controlled recurrent alternatives?

The 55 trained models and 440 model-by-benchmark-by-split evaluation cells are
separate units: 55 training runs, each scored on 4 benchmarks x 2 splits. Of
those 440 scores, 438 are complete in this snapshot. Every cell in Table 1
comes from a score manifest; the underlying task scores remain in the results
tree. Table 2 averages only across available trained seeds. These descriptive
averages do not constitute paired hypothesis tests.

2. Data, directions, and eligibility
------------------------------------
Languages: Python, Java, C++. Six directed pairs: C++->Java, C++->Python,
Java->C++, Java->Python, Python->C++, Python->Java. The supervised corpus is
XLCoST program-level translation with a fixed training/validation split.
Training overlap with external benchmark problems was audited and removed
before the confirmatory matrix. Benchmark task identities, references, and
execution tests were frozen before model prediction. Pretrained-backbone
contamination cannot be ruled out by this project audit.

Benchmark                 Common-Core tasks   Full-Directional tasks   Role
------------------------  ------------------  -----------------------  ---------
xCodeEval                 1992                3024                     primary
HumanEval-X                942                 956                     secondary
Avatar-TC                 3298                3298                     auxiliary
TransCoder-Test           1926                1936                     secondary

Each count is per trained model. Common-Core and Full-Directional are separate
predeclared task sets and are not pooled as independent observations. Their
cases may overlap. xCodeEval is evaluated by the project's isolated execution
harness, not the official ExecEval service. Avatar-TC has one published input
per problem, which limits what a passing result establishes. See the frozen
eligibility manifests in data/manifests/ and audit reports in reports/data/.

3. Translation and decoding protocol
------------------------------------
The shared prompt requests translation of a complete source program, displays
an optional official target interface, then the source program, and asks for
the target program. Every architecture on a backbone uses the same prompt,
training targets, and output parser. The target interface is supplied only
when the benchmark provides it; HumanEval-X uses its official declaration.
Only newly generated tokens are used. Line endings are normalized. A single
whole-output Markdown code fence may be removed; code is not otherwise
repaired. Invalid, incomplete, or noncompiling generated code is scored as
produced.

Protocol v3 generates one independently seeded stochastic candidate per task
(temperature 0.8, top-p 0.95, one beam, maximum 768 new tokens for the 7B
Qwen run; exact per-backbone settings are in the frozen protocol). A task has
Pass@1 = 1 only when that candidate passes every frozen eligible test, else 0.
The directional score is the arithmetic mean over eligible tasks. The macro
score in Tables 1-3 is the unweighted mean of available directional scores:

    Pass@1_macro(b) = (1/|D_b|) * sum_{d in D_b} Pass@1_(b,d).

Here |D_b|=6 for xCodeEval, HumanEval-X, and TransCoder-Test, and 2 for
Avatar-TC (Java->Python and Python->Java). Scores across benchmarks are not
a single pooled six-direction result.

Protocol v1 greedy and partial Protocol v2 five-candidate generations were
archived and excluded. Pass@5 is not measured in Protocol v3. XLCoST validation
also records BLEU-4, CodeBLEU, exact match, and CrystalBLEU as reference-based
diagnostics. Validation target loss, not any external benchmark score or
lexical metric, selects checkpoints.

4. Shared training design
-------------------------
The confirmatory matrix is 40 Qwen2.5-Coder-7B runs, 9 Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B
runs, and 6 single-seed family/scale runs (Qwen2.5-Coder-14B and
StarCoder2-7B). The primary 7B matrix contains ten architectures trained at
seeds 13, 42, and 87, plus ten additional architectures screened at seed 42.
The 1.5B matrix trains Dense, MoR-style, and Full-SART at three seeds. The
14B and StarCoder2 matrices train those same three architectures at seed 42.
Two convergence pilots lie outside the 55-run matrix. The common horizon is
two epochs, selected from in-domain validation-only pilots. The best checkpoint
within that horizon is selected by validation target loss. The context limit
for 7B training is 2048 tokens; overlength examples are excluded rather than
truncated. The 7B generation cap was chosen from the training/validation
length distribution, not external outputs.

The decoder-only backbone is adapted with LoRA on q/k/v/o attention projections
and gate/up/down MLP projections. Resolved training defaults: LoRA rank 16,
alpha 32, dropout 0.05; recurrent width 1024, maximum recurrence Rmax=6,
insertion fraction 0.5 (roughly the middle decoder layer); microbatch 1,
gradient accumulation 16, AdamW weight decay 0.1, warmup fraction 0.04,
gradient clipping norm 1. The backbone/LoRA learning rate is 1e-5 and the
newly initialized adapter learning rate is 5e-5 in the RunConfig defaults.
The resolved YAML and checkpoint metadata for each run are authoritative if
an individual run differs. A separate last-backbone-layer unfreezing study was
planned pending a memory profile; its planned setting must not be treated as
completed evidence.

The recurrent adapter acts on source-prefix hidden states at the insertion
point. Target tokens are predicted autoregressively by the ordinary decoder;
there is no separate target-side recurrent decision module. Dense uses no
inserted recurrent adapter. The parameter-matched Dense control uses a
nonrecurrent residual bottleneck adapter.

5. Semantic regions and graph
-----------------------------
Tree-sitter supplies source syntax spans. The extractor maps language-specific
syntax to 22 normalized region types: PROGRAM, FUNCTION, PARAMETER,
DECLARATION, CONDITIONAL, LOOP, SWITCH, BRANCH, ASSIGNMENT, MUTATION, CALL,
CONSTRUCTOR, MEMBER_ACCESS, INDEX_ACCESS, ARITHMETIC, COMPARISON,
BOOLEAN_EXPRESSION, RETURN, BREAK, CONTINUE, EXCEPTION, OTHER_STRUCTURAL.
Tokens map deterministically to one region or are marked unmapped. Mapped
tokens are pooled into a region vector by mean pooling, except in the
attention-pooling ablation. The graph uses directed typed AST_PARENT,
AST_CHILD, CFG_NEXT, CFG_TRUE, CFG_FALSE, CFG_BACKEDGE, DFG_DEF_USE, and
DFG_USE_DEF relations where extracted. Missing relations are not fabricated.
Joern-derived control/data-flow edges are projected onto these regions.
Region scalars are AST depth, log(1+child count), log(1+CFG degree),
log(1+DFG degree), and log(1+region length).

For region i with mapped token set T_i and source hidden states h_t:

    r_i = (1/|T_i|) * sum_{t in T_i} h_t       [mean-pooling variants]
    z_i^0 = W_in r_i.

For typed edge i<-j of relation e, the graph module forms query q_i=W_q z_i,
key k_ij=W_k z_j+E_key(e), and value v_ij=W_v z_j+E_val(e). It computes
attention alpha_ij=softmax_j(q_i*k_ij/sqrt(width)) over incoming neighbors and
m_i=LayerNorm(W_o * sum_j alpha_ij v_ij). Graph-free controls use m_i=0.

6. Recurrent update and the depth-predicting neural network
-----------------------------------------------------------
The general name for the depth predictor is an adaptive-computation router,
halting network, or gating network. This implementation calls it `router` for
regions and `token_router` for MoR-style tokens. It is a learned multilayer
perceptron (MLP), not a separate transformer. The region router has:

    LayerNorm -> Linear(input, 1024) -> GELU -> Linear(1024, 1) -> sigmoid.

Its input at each step concatenates current region representation z_i^k,
graph message m_i^k, region-type embedding, source/target language-pair
embedding, and five scalar structural features. For graph-free regional
routing, structure inputs are zeroed. The token router uses current token
state, a zeroed type slot, and language-pair embedding; its hidden width is
max(128, 1024/4)=256. The step embedding enters the recurrent state update,
not the router input. Router temperature divides the final logit before the
sigmoid. With q_i^k as probability of *continuing* after step k:

    q_i^k = sigmoid(MLP([z_i^k, m_i^k, type_i, pair, features_i]) / T).

The shared recurrent transformation uses a residual MLP:

    u_i^k = LayerNorm(z_i^k + W_2 GELU(W_1[z_i^k,m_i^k,type_i,pair]))
            + step_embedding_k + W_feature features_i.

During soft training, z_i^{k+1}=z_i^k+a_i^k q_i^k(u_i^k-z_i^k), where a_i^k
is the probability of still being active before this step; the survival
probability is updated by a_i^{k+1}=a_i^k q_i^k. In calibrated hard inference,
q_i^k is thresholded (or selected by top-k where configured) and only active
indices are updated. Thresholds are chosen on XLCoST validation using the
seed-42 checkpoint, then shared across seeds of one architecture/backbone.
The configured Rmax is six; stopping after the final update has no additional
compute effect. The final region delta is W_out(z_i^R-z_i^0), scattered to
mapped source tokens and added as a scaled residual to h_t. Unmapped tokens
receive no region delta. The scale parameter initializes at 1e-3.

Expected depth is the sum of survival probabilities:

    E[D_i] = sum_{k=1}^{Rmax} prod_{j=1}^{k-1} q_i^j,
    mean_depth = sum_i mask_i E[D_i] / sum_i mask_i,
    L_budget = (mean_depth - B)^2,
    L_train = L_target_cross_entropy + lambda * L_budget.

The default budget B is 3.0 and lambda is 0.1. For uniform token/region loops,
q=1 through all six steps. Structure-Only performs one structural update.
These equations summarize the implementation; exact per-run settings and the
hard-routing branch are in src/models/recurrent/region_module.py and frozen
routing policies in reports/evaluation/frozen_external_protocol.json.

8. Statistical methods and what is established
----------------------------------------------
The preregistered xCodeEval Common-Core family uses paired problem-cluster
contrasts of Full-SART with Dense, Uniform Token Loop, MoR-style, Random
Region, and Structure-Only. The three-seed comparison matches problem IDs
and all six directions. For two models A and B, the equal-direction effect is

    Delta = mean_seed,problem,direction(Pass@1_A - Pass@1_B).

The hierarchical bootstrap resamples seeds and problem IDs, retaining all
six directions for each sampled problem, to form a 95% interval. The paired
randomization test swaps A/B labels once per problem cluster (shared across
seeds and directions) for a two-sided p-value. Holm correction applies across
the five preregistered comparisons. Exact McNemar tests by seed/direction are
secondary diagnostics, not substitutes for the clustered primary contrast.
Table 4 shows those five comparisons, including a negative Full-SART versus
Random Region contrast; no core-model superiority claim is established by
that table. See the complete machine-readable statistical record for all
secondary direction/seed details.

Table 5 adds four direct Uniform Token Loop contrasts requested after results
were inspected. They form a separate exploratory Holm family. Their intervals
resample seeds and problems, while their randomization p-values swap labels
by problem. These methods quantify different uncertainty: the looped model's
small positive mean versus Dense has a wide interval and high adjusted p.
The negative mean versus Random Region and Region-Adaptive-No-Graph has
adjusted problem-randomization p<0.05, but the seed-aware intervals cross
zero. This does not establish seed-robust underperformance. No confirmatory
claim about the looped model versus these comparators is justified yet.

APPENDIX C. WHY EACH EXPERIMENT WAS CONDUCTED
=============================================

C1. Main causal ladder and controls
-----------------------------------
The central question is whether structural guidance and adaptive depth add
value beyond ordinary computation. Each comparison changes a more specific
factor, while holding the dataset, translation directions, prompt, training
horizon, decoding protocol, and backbone fixed within a matched run group.
A descriptive difference alone cannot identify a cause when compute or
parameters also differ; measured compute and paired inference are needed.

Experiment                    Why it was conducted / question answered
----------------------------  ---------------------------------------------------------
Dense                         Establish ordinary transformer translation quality,
                              training cost, and inference cost without recurrence.
Dense-ParamMatch              Ask whether any gain or loss is explained merely by
                              extra trainable adapter capacity rather than recurrence.
Uniform Token Loop            Ask whether shared repeated token refinement helps
                              even when every mapped token receives equal depth.
MoR-style                     Ask whether token-level learned adaptive depth adds
                              value beyond uniform token recurrence; isolate routing
                              without source semantic regions or graph relations.
Uniform Region Loop           Ask whether region pooling and recurrent regional
                              updates help without adaptive halting.
RegionAdaptive-NoGraph        Ask whether region-level adaptive routing alone helps,
                              without typed graph messages or structural router cues.
Random Region                 Ask whether the benefit comes from genuine semantic
                              regions rather than arbitrary region grouping.
Structure-Only                Ask whether one structural graph update explains an
                              effect attributed to repeated adaptive computation.
Full-SART                     Test the complete proposal: semantic regions, typed
                              AST/CFG/DFG graph messages, and region-adaptive depth.

Useful direct contrasts and their specific interpretation:

Contrast                                Factor under examination
--------------------------------------  ----------------------------------------------
Uniform Token Loop - Dense              Uniform recurrence beyond backbone computation
MoR-style - Uniform Token Loop          Learned token halting beyond fixed recurrence
Uniform Region Loop - Uniform Token     Region pooling/granularity under uniform depth
RegionAdaptive-NoGraph - Uniform Region Region halting beyond uniform region recurrence
Full-SART - RegionAdaptive-NoGraph      Structural graph/features beyond region halting
Full-SART - Structure-Only              Repeated adaptive updates beyond one graph pass
Full-SART - Random Region               Semantic structure beyond random grouping
Full-SART - Dense-ParamMatch            Structure/recurrence beyond added parameters

The listed contrasts explain the design. Only contrasts for which paired tests
have actually been computed should be described as statistically analyzed.
The completed paired tests are in Tables 4 and 5. The other contrasts are
motivations or descriptive comparisons until direct paired analyses are run.

C2. Structural component and representation ablations
------------------------------------------------------
These interventions are designed to locate which source of structural
information, or which part of the recurrent module, matters. They are
mechanism probes, not independent proof of a causal mechanism. Several are
single-seed screens and should not be generalized across training seeds.

Experiment                    Why it was conducted / question answered
----------------------------  ---------------------------------------------------------
AST-SART                      Test whether syntax containment alone supplies enough
                              structural guidance compared with Full-SART.
CFG-SART                      Test the contribution of control-flow relations alone.
DFG-SART                      Test the contribution of data-flow relations alone.
AST+CFG-SART                  Test syntax plus control-flow without data-flow edges.
AST+DFG-SART                  Test syntax plus data-flow without control-flow edges.
Shuffled-Structure            Check whether valid structural alignment matters, as
                              opposed to graph features acting as generic noise.
SART-No-Scalar                Test whether explicit numeric structural features
                              contribute beyond region embeddings and graph messages.
SART-No-Message               Test the contribution of typed graph message passing
                              while retaining the other recurrent components.
SART-Attention-Pool           Probe whether learned weighted token-to-region pooling
                              improves on mean pooling or reduces information loss.
SART-No-Language-Pair         Test whether source/target language conditioning in the
                              module matters beyond the language names in the prompt.
SART-Coarse-Ontology          Test whether fine semantic region categories are
                              necessary or a coarser taxonomy suffices.

Why these tests matter: a Full-SART difference could arise from region pooling,
extra parameters, typed graph edges, scalar features, or the learned depth
policy. The controls are intended to separate these explanations. Inference
should respect their available seed count and measured compute. The attention
pooling ablation tests one pooling choice; learned region tokens and a
persistent token-plus-region recurrent state were proposed as future work and
were not part of these trained comparisons.

C3. Seeds, model sizes, and model family
---------------------------------------
Group                         Why it was conducted / question answered
----------------------------  ---------------------------------------------------------
7B seeds 13, 42, 87           Measure variation from training randomness and enable
                              paired problem/seed comparisons for ten core methods.
7B single-seed screens        Examine more structural components within the available
                              compute budget; results are exploratory.
Qwen 1.5B, three seeds        Ask whether Dense/MoR/Full-SART behavior changes at a
                              smaller backbone size while retaining seed replication.
Qwen 14B, seed 42             Ask whether the core pattern transfers to a larger
                              backbone; one seed limits uncertainty estimates.
StarCoder2 7B, seed 42        Ask whether the pattern transfers beyond the Qwen model
                              family; one seed limits uncertainty estimates.
Convergence pilots            Select the shared two-epoch horizon using XLCoST
                              validation before external test results existed.

The different backbone checks are replication probes. A score difference
between backbones is not a clean architecture effect because pretraining,
parameter count, and tokenizer also differ. Compare architectures within the
same backbone first.

C4. Benchmark and split rationale
---------------------------------
Benchmark / split             Why it was conducted / question answered
----------------------------  ---------------------------------------------------------
xCodeEval Common-Core         Primary external functional test on matched problems
                              across all six directions; main paired inference set.
xCodeEval Full-Directional    Check whether findings persist on the larger eligible
                              directional task set; report separately from core.
HumanEval-X                   Independent execution benchmark with target-language
                              callable declarations; test benchmark transfer.
TransCoder-Test               Independent function translation benchmark with its
                              official f_gold/f_filled harness; test transfer and
                              expose interface or compilation failures.
Avatar-TC                     Auxiliary whole-program Java/Python execution check;
                              narrower test coverage limits interpretation.
Common-Core                   Keep a balanced problem set for paired six-direction
                              analysis, reducing changes in problem composition.
Full-Directional              Use all eligible direction-specific tasks and reveal
                              whether a balanced-core result holds more broadly.

All architectures receive the same frozen task definition and candidate
scoring rule for a given benchmark. Primary scores are execution Pass@1, not
BLEU-like similarity. Scores near zero or large benchmark differences require
harness and task-level inspection before explaining the cause.

C5. Diagnostics and future hypotheses
--------------------------------------
Diagnostic / proposal         Why it matters and present status
----------------------------  ---------------------------------------------------------
Depth histogram and entropy   Detect router collapse to minimum or maximum depth and
                              show whether learned computation is genuinely adaptive.
Depth by region/direction     Test whether routing changes with program structure or
                              language pair, rather than only overall mean depth.
Depth vs frozen difficulty    Test association between routing and pre-model
                              structural difficulty; association is not causation.
Validation learning curves    Check whether new modules had converged under the
                              frozen horizon; external scores must not retune it.
Parameter/FLOP/latency audit  Distinguish quality from compute cost and locate the
                              accuracy-compute frontier at measured inference cost.
StructLoop proposal           Future fixed-depth, structure-conditioned recurrence:
                              ask whether structure should guide updates while all
                              units retain uniform depth. It has not been trained or
                              evaluated in this matrix. Its xCodeEval motivation is
                              post-hoc; future confirmation needs separate data.

The current tables answer which score cells exist and what the calculated
xCodeEval Common-Core comparisons show. They do not yet answer whether
Full-SART is more compute-efficient, whether its router tracks difficulty,
or whether any architecture generalizes a statistically significant effect
across all external benchmarks. Those require the outstanding analyses.
